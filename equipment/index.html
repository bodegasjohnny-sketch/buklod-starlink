Plan a site at ₱150,000.

Starlink Standard Kit

The satellite link. One kit per site.

₱28,000 – ₱35,000

Local Priority / Business plan only. Residential resale is prohibited. Clear sky matters more than a pretty roof.

Mount and pole

Holds the dish and, often, the sector.

₱2,000 – ₱5,000

Build the mount before the crew arrives.

Outdoor sector antenna

Spreads the link across about a kilometer.

₱15,000 – ₱25,000

5 GHz is the practical default through trees. 60 GHz wants a cleaner line of sight and a shorter hop. The sector, not the dish, is what the houses actually see.

Outdoor access point

Point-to-multipoint base station.

₱12,000 – ₱20,000

Weatherproof, with a drip loop. This is the radio that fails in a surge. Dress the cable. Water follows a sloppy run into the port.

Client radios (CPE)

For houses at the edge, or behind a building.

₱3,000 – ₱6,000

Optional per house. Stock a few. Near houses can use an ordinary router if the signal is honest.

PoE, injectors, cable

Power and data up the pole.

₱3,000 – ₱5,000

Carry a spare injector. It is the part lightning eats first.

Surge protector and arrestor

Keeps a storm from becoming a new radio.

₱1,500 – ₱3,000

Ground it. An arrestor with no ground is a sticker.

Power — grid and UPS, or solar and battery

The link is useless in the dark.

₱15,000 – ₱40,000

The wide range is the difference between a UPS and a real off-grid kit. Size the battery for a string of rain days, not a brochure noon.

Weatherproof enclosure

Router, injector, and arrestor live here.

₱2,000 – ₱4,000

Lock it. The host fee is partly for watching it.

Install materials

Clamps, tape, ties, sealant, grounding.

₱2,000 – ₱4,000

Cheap, and the usual reason a “quick” job runs long.

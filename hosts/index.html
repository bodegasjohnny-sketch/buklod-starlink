The roof, not the business

Free internet, a monthly fee, and none of the equipment.

You keep the hardware and all of the voucher revenue. The host keeps a roof over a live link, free service for their own use, and a fixed cash amount. That is the model the guide recommends for a rollout you can finance.

A good host is elevation, power, and someone who will notice if the enclosure is open.

Why not split the revenue

A share of ₱50,000 sounds generous and gets messy the month collections dip. A fee is boring. Boring is what you want on a thousand roofs.

Why you own the gear

If the host sells the house or changes their mind, the dish, the radio, and the solar kit leave with you. Investors can underwrite that. They cannot underwrite a box in someone else’s name.

What “good location” means

Clear sky, a view over roofs for about a kilometer, and power. The fee goes up when the roof is actually elevated, not when the household is merely willing.

What to offer

The planning model uses ₱2,000 a month, inside the guide’s ₱1,500–₱2,500 band for a solid site. Match the fee to the roof, not to how much you like the host.

Free internet + ₱1,500–2,000 / month
Sari-sari, mini-mart, eatery
Free internet + ₱2,000–3,500 / month
Barangay hall, school, clinic
Free internet for the facility + ₱1,000–2,000 / month

Put it in writing

  • Operator owns every piece of equipment, including CPEs you supplied.
  • Host receives free internet and a fixed monthly fee, not a share of voucher revenue.
  • Host gives access for installs and repairs on reasonable notice, and immediately if the site is dark.
  • Host keeps the enclosure locked and tells you the same day something is missing or opened.
  • Fee stops if you remove the gear. You can remove it if the roof becomes unusable or the fee is disputed.
  • Either side can end the arrangement in writing. Gear leaves with the operator.

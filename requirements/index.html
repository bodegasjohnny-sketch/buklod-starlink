Before you charge anyone

Register as a service, not as a hobby with a dish.

A pilot on a friend’s roof is how you learn the signal. Charging a barangay is a value-added service. The guide’s best practice is to be inside the VAS or Konektadong Pinoy / DTIP frame before the rollout, not after the first complaint.

Residential Starlink is the wrong plan. Resale of a residential plan is prohibited. Use Local Priority or Business. The 500 GB tier is ₱4,650. The 1 TB tier is ₱8,400. If a residential dish “works fine,” it still does not make the vouchers legal.

Mayor’s permit

The LGU where the business is registered. Some sites also need a local clearance to mount on a public building.

03

BIR registration

Form 2303, books, and official receipts before you are taking voucher money at any scale.

Standard

04

NTC VAS or DTIP

Required if you charge users for internet. This is the registration that makes the model legal, not optional paperwork for later.

Core fees about ₱6,000–₱10,000, plus possible minimum capitalization

05

Barangay clearance

Per site or for the business, depending on the barangay. Cheap, and politically useful.

Low

06

Starlink plan class

Local Priority or Business only. Residential resale is prohibited. Plan changes take effect next billing cycle.

₱4,650 (500 GB) or ₱8,400 (1 TB) per site each month

Fees and capitalization rules move. Treat the ₱6,000–₱10,000 core-fee range as a 2026 planning number, then read the current NTC schedule before you file. A stop-order is the expensive version of this page.

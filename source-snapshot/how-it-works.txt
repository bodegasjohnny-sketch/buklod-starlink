How a barangay gets online
One dish, a sector, a book of vouchers.
The link is a Starlink Local Priority plan. The coverage is an outdoor point-to-multipoint radio. The business is 50 to 100 households paying monthly, on equipment you still own.
1 km
Schematic, not a survey. The amber dot is the host roof. Navy dots are households inside about a kilometer. Trees, a water tank, or a hill will move the real edge.
The sky
The dish needs a clear view. A mango canopy is a bad host, however friendly the household.
The kilometer
A 5 GHz sector is the default through trees and houses. 60 GHz is faster and fussier — cleaner line of sight, shorter hop.
The edge
Houses near the roof often connect with an ordinary router. Houses at the edge get a small client radio, ₱3,000–₱6,000 each.
The portal
Prepaid vouchers, sold by the host or a sari-sari. Keep the price inside the guide’s ₱400–₱600 band.

Operating rules
01
Own the equipment
You buy it, you mount it, you can move it. Host-owned hardware feels cheaper until the first dispute, and it is a poor story for investors.
02
Write the host agreement
Free internet, the monthly fee, access, security of the gear, and what happens when either side stops. A handshake does not survive a family argument.
03
Solve power before the dish arrives
Stable grid plus a UPS, or solar and battery in the budget. A perfect radio on a dead breaker is a dark site.
04
Pick elevation and a clear sky
The dish wants sky. The sector wants a view of roofs, not a wall of mango trees. Walk the barangay before you sign the host.
05
Pilot 10–30 sites
Learn permits, fill-up time, and which host type actually watches the roof. Then scale. The guide is explicit about this.
06
Upgrade the plan as the book grows
500 GB while the site is filling. 1 TB as it gets busy. Watch the usage; do not wait for the complaints.
07
Use a simple voucher portal
Prepaid, monthly, sold by the host or a nearby store. Keep the price inside ₱400–₱600.
08
Name a local pair of hands
One or two people per area who can power-cycle a radio, reseat a cable, and tell you the truth. You cannot fly a crew for every brownout.
09
Raise against the build, not the brochure
The guide’s raise is ₱180–200 million for about 1,000 sites, with 8–10 investors and a suggested 40–50% of profit only once the network is stable in year two. The risks page shows why the buffer exists.
10
Choose barangays, not pins on a map
High demand, a cooperative LGU, a host with a real roof, and no decent incumbent signal. Skip the rest until the crew has a rhythm.
